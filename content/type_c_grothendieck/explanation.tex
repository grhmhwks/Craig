\section{Purpose and status}

This note records computational evidence for a conjectural positive expansion
of type \(C\) \(K\)-Stanley symmetric functions.  The conjecture appears in
the paper of Arroyo, Hamaker, Hawkes, and Pan~\cite{AHHP}.  The code included
here checks a stronger, peakset-refined finite statement.  The checks are
exact enumerations in bounded rank and degree; they do not construct the
conjectural bijection and do not prove the general expansion.

We use the standard convention that the type \(C\) Weyl group \(W_a\) has
simple generators
\[
  s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_{a-1}.
\]
Thus the alphabet for \(W_a\) is \(\{0,1,\ldots,a-1\}\).

\section{Hecke words and peaksets}

An element of \(W_a\) is a signed permutation
\(w=[w_1,\ldots,w_a]\).  In the type \(C\) \(0\)-Hecke monoid the Coxeter
relations are retained and the relation \(s_i^2=1\) is replaced by
\(s_i\circ s_i=s_i\).  A word
\[
  u=(u_1,\ldots,u_n),\qquad u_i\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\},
\]
is a \emph{Hecke word} for \(w\) when
\[
  w=s_{u_1}\circ\cdots\circ s_{u_n}.
\]
Its peakset is
\[
  \Peak(u)=\{i:2\le i\le n-1,\ u_{i-1}<u_i>u_{i+1}\}.
\]
The reduced finite check uses the subset \(\mathcal H_n^*(w)\) consisting of
Hecke words with no adjacent equal letters.

The type \(C\) stable Grothendieck polynomial, also called the type \(C\)
\(K\)-Stanley symmetric function, is denoted \(\GC_w\).  It may be described
as a generating function over signed unimodal factorizations of Hecke words;
the monomial records the factor sizes.  This definition is equivalent to the
coefficient definition in Section~3 of~\cite{AHHP}.

\section{The two tableau families}

A strict partition \(\lambda=(\lambda_1>\cdots>\lambda_k>0)\) determines a
shifted Young diagram.  Two tableau families of the same strict shape occur in
the conjecture.

\subsection*{Strict decomposition tableaux}

A word \(r_1\cdots r_m\) is \emph{unimodal} if, for some dip position \(q\),
\[
  r_1>\cdots>r_q<r_{q+1}<\cdots<r_m.
\]
A strict decomposition tableau is a shifted tableau whose rows are unimodal
and whose adjacent rows satisfy the type \(C\) column condition of
Definition~4.3 in~\cite{AHHP}.  Briefly, after the decreasing portions of the
two rows are signed, the first and last entries of the lower row must lie
below the first entry of the upper row, and no signed entry outside a column
may lie in the forbidden interval determined by the two entries in that
column.  The function \texttt{column\_compatible} in \texttt{code.py}
implements this condition directly.

Rows are read left to right, from the bottom row to the top row.  Let
\(\SDT_w(\lambda)\) be the set of strict decomposition tableaux of shape
\(\lambda\) whose reading words are Hecke words for \(w\), and put
\[
  a_w^C(\lambda)=|\SDT_w(\lambda)|.
\]
These objects were called type \(C\) unimodal Hecke tableaux in the earlier
computational formulation.

\subsection*{Standard shifted set-valued tableaux}

A standard shifted set-valued tableau \(Q\) of shape \(\lambda\) fills the
boxes of the shifted diagram with nonempty sets that partition
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with entries weakly increasing across rows and down
columns in the set-valued sense.  The integer \(n\) is the number of labels,
not the number of boxes.  Its peakset is
\[
  \Peak(Q)=\{i: i-1\text{ lies in a column left of }i,
                 \text{ and }i+1\text{ lies in a row below }i\}.
\]
The reduced check further requires that \(i\) and \(i+1\) never occur in the
same box.

The \(Q\)-Grothendieck function \(GQ_\lambda^{(\beta)}\) is the generating
function of semistandard shifted set-valued tableaux of shape \(\lambda\),
with \(\beta\) recording the excess number of entries over boxes.

\section{The conjectural expansions}

The main conjecture of~\cite{AHHP} is the following.

\begin{conjecture}[Arroyo--Hamaker--Hawkes--Pan]
For every signed permutation \(w\),
\[
  \GC_w
  =
  \sum_{\lambda\text{ strict}}
     \beta^{|\lambda|-\ell(w)}
     a_w^C(\lambda)\,GQ_\lambda^{(\beta)}.
\]
Equivalently, the coefficient of \(GQ_\lambda^{(\beta)}\) is counted by
strict decomposition tableaux of shape \(\lambda\) whose reading word is a
Hecke word for \(w\).
\end{conjecture}

A useful normalized form separates the sign choices on the factorization side
from the priming choices on the tableau side.  Let \(\GC_w^+\) retain only
factorizations in which every factor has a unique entry of least absolute
value and that entry has positive sign.  Let \(GR_\lambda\) be the
corresponding submodel of \(GQ_\lambda\) in which the first occurrence of
\(i\) or \(i'\), in the prescribed reading order, is the unprimed \(i\).  The
stronger normalized prediction is schematically
\[
  \GC_w^+=\sum_{P\in\SDT_w}\beta^{|P|-\ell(w)}GR_{\shape(P)}.
\]
Removing these parallel normalizations gives the \(\GC_w\)-to-\(GQ\)
expansion.  Thus the normalized statement contains the unrestricted one.

\section{The reduced peakset statement}

The computational backbone is smaller than either full symmetric-function
identity.  For a signed permutation \(w\), degree \(n\), and peakset \(S\),
define
\[
  \mathcal W(w,n,S)
  =\{u\in\mathcal H_n^*(w):\Peak(u)=S\}
\]
and
\[
  \mathcal P(w,n,S)
  =\left\{(P,Q):
  \begin{array}{l}
    P\in\SDT_w(\lambda)\text{ for some strict }\lambda,\\
    Q\in\ShSet_n(\lambda),\quad \Peak(Q)=S,\\
    \text{no box of }Q\text{ contains consecutive labels}
  \end{array}\right\}.
\]
The strongest finite comparison is
\[
  |\mathcal W(w,n,S)|=|\mathcal P(w,n,S)|
  \qquad\text{for every }(w,n,S)
\]
in the selected rank and degree range.

This is the natural standardized form of the conjecture.  Standardization of
shifted set-valued tableaux organizes \(GQ\) into peak quasisymmetric
functions, while adjacent repetitions in a Hecke word correspond to
consecutive recording labels sharing a box.  Consequently, a
peakset-preserving bijection between the reduced objects would inflate to the
normalized \((G_w^C)^+\)-to-\(GR\) correspondence, and then to the unrestricted
\(GC_w\)-to-\(GQ\) expansion.  The program verifies the resulting
peakset-refined equinumerosity in finite ranges; it does not supply the
bijection.

\section{What the Python program checks}

The program constructs three count tables.

\paragraph{Word table.}
It grows repetition-free words one letter at a time.  Storing all words would
be wasteful, so partial words with identical continuation data are merged.
A state records the current signed permutation, the last letter, whether the
previous comparison was a rise, and the peakset bitmask.  Appending a generator updates the \(0\)-Hecke product,
forbids repetition of the last letter, and decides whether the previous
position becomes a peak.  The result is
\[
  W[w][(n,S)]=|\mathcal W(w,n,S)|.
\]

\paragraph{Recording-tableau table.}
Starting from the empty tableau, the next label is placed either in a valid
existing outer-corner box or in a new outer-corner box.  Consecutive labels
are forbidden in the same box, and states with the same shape, last two
positions, and peakset are merged.  This gives
\[
  R[\lambda][(n,S)]
  =\#\{Q\in\ShSet_n(\lambda):\Peak(Q)=S,
            \text{ no consecutive labels share a box}\}.
\]

\paragraph{Hecke-tableau table.}
The code first generates all unimodal rows over \(\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\), then
builds strict decomposition tableaux row by row using the column condition.
It records
\[
  H[w][\lambda]=|\SDT_w(\lambda)|.
\]

\paragraph{Comparison.}
The tableau-pair count is obtained without explicitly constructing the
Cartesian product:
\[
  P[w][(n,S)]
  =\sum_\lambda H[w][\lambda]R[\lambda][(n,S)].
\]
The program compares \(W[w][(n,S)]\) and \(P[w][(n,S)]\) for every key.
It uses only the Python standard library.

For example,
\begin{verbatim}
python code.py --rank 4 --max-degree 12
\end{verbatim}
checks \(W_4\), with generators \(0,1,2,3\), through degree \(12\).
This run passed with no mismatched peakset bins.

\section{Recorded evidence}

The paper reports that Conjecture~1.3 was checked through degree \(9\) for
\(w\in W_4\) and through degree \(11\) for \(w\in W_3\)~\cite{AHHP}.
The readable Python implementation supplied here was additionally run for
\(W_4\) through degree \(12\), again with no mismatches.

For larger experiments, the item also includes
\texttt{type\_c\_peakset\_check.cpp}.  Its internal optimizations are not needed to
understand the check.  Compiled with
\begin{verbatim}
g++ -O3 -march=native -DNDEBUG -std=c++20 -pthread \
    type_c_peakset_check.cpp -o type_c_peakset_check
\end{verbatim}
and run as
\begin{verbatim}
./type_c_peakset_check 15 4
\end{verbatim}
it verified \(W_6\), with generators \(0,\ldots,5\), through degree \(15\).
The degree-zero case is the common empty object and is immediate.  The run
reported
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{repetition-free words counted} &=& 45{,}776{,}367{,}186,\\
\text{strict decomposition tableaux} &=& 915{,}518,\\
\text{eligible recording tableaux} &=& 3{,}031{,}815,\\
\text{nonzero peakset bins compared} &=& 14{,}441{,}690,\\
\text{mismatches} &=& 0.
\end{array}
\]
On the machine used for this item, the wall time was approximately
\(2.82\) seconds and peak resident memory was approximately \(636\) MiB.
Runtime is machine-dependent.

\section{Interpretation}

The computations provide substantial evidence for a peakset-preserving
correspondence, and hence for the conjectural \(GR\) and \(GQ\) expansions of type \(C\) \(K\)-Stanley functions.
They establish exact equality of all reduced finite bins in the stated ranges,
but they do not explain the equality.  The central open problem remains to
construct a peakset-preserving bijection
\[
  u\longleftrightarrow(P,Q)
\]
between repetition-free type \(C\) Hecke words and compatible pairs of strict
decomposition and shifted set-valued recording tableaux.
